## 🏛️ Politics & Governance

### State of Emergency Marks Four Years
President Nayib Bukele's state of emergency — declared in April 2022 as part of his sweeping anti-gang crackdown — has now entered its fourth year. The measure has led to approximately **91,000 detentions** since its inception. Human Rights Watch (HRW) has called on Bukele's government to end the emergency, citing widespread human rights violations. A humanitarian legal aid NGO has reported **504 detainee deaths** in Salvadoran prisons between April 2022 and March 2026.

### Life Sentences Approved — Including for Minors
In late March 2026, El Salvador's Legislative Assembly approved a constitutional amendment allowing **life imprisonment** for crimes including murder, rape, and terrorism. A separate reform also amended the juvenile criminal law to permit life sentences for minors as young as **12 years old** who are linked to criminal groups. The United Nations Office of the High Commissioner for Human Rights urged El Salvador to reconsider the juvenile measure, calling it incompatible with international human rights standards.

### Vamos Party Steps Back from 2027 Race
The political party Vamos announced it will **not nominate a presidential candidate** in the 2027 presidential election, signaling a significant shift in Salvadoran opposition politics ahead of the next cycle.

A Venezuelan man deported from the United States and held in El Salvador's **CECOT mega-prison** (Terrorist Confinement Centre) has filed a federal lawsuit seeking at least **$1.3 million in damages**, alleging abuse amounting to torture during his detention.

## 💰 Economy & Bitcoin

### Bitcoin Strategy Continues Despite Slump
El Salvador continues to purchase **one Bitcoin per day**, maintaining approximately **7,517 BTC** in national reserves — valued at over **$660 million** at current prices. Despite a broader Bitcoin market slump that critics say has "shattered" Bukele's digital utopia, the government remains committed to its crypto-forward strategy.

### IMF Praises Economic Growth
The International Monetary Fund praised El Salvador's economic progress, projecting **real GDP growth of around 4%** for 2026. Tensions between the government and IMF over El Salvador's Bitcoin holdings have eased following policy adjustments made in 2025, when Bitcoin was removed as official legal tender. The country is now working toward integrating traditional finance with digital assets through APIs and private blockchains.

### Nuclear Energy Partnership with the U.S.
In March 2026, El Salvador and the United States signed an agreement (**Agreement 123**) to cooperate on the development of a **nuclear power plant** in El Salvador, representing a major energy infrastructure milestone for the country.
