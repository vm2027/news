## 1. US–El Salvador Deportation Controversy & CECOT Prison

One of the most prominent ongoing stories involves El Salvador's role in the Trump administration's immigration enforcement strategy. Since March 2025, the US has deported over 260 migrants—primarily Venezuelans—to El Salvador's notorious CECOT (Centro de Confinamiento del Terrorismo) mega-prison, under an agreement reportedly worth $4.8–6 million.

Detainees reported systematic beatings by guards for minor infractions such as speaking loudly, showering off schedule, or requesting medical care. Human rights organizations Human Rights Watch and Cristosal concluded that treatment of Venezuelan deportees amounted to **arbitrary detention and enforced disappearance** under international law.

Legal battles have followed. A Venezuelan man deported from the US and held at CECOT filed a **$1.3 million federal lawsuit** alleging torture. US courts have also weighed in, with Judge James Boasberg ordering the Trump administration to submit plans to return or provide hearings for migrants sent to CECOT.

President Bukele has been firm that El Salvador will not return deportees based on US court orders, maintaining this is a matter of Salvadoran sovereignty. Bukele met with President Trump at the White House in April 2026, reaffirming security and migration cooperation between the two countries.

## 2. Security & Gang Crackdown

El Salvador's state of exception—declared in March 2022 after a surge in gang violence—has now been extended **49 consecutive times** as of March 26, 2026. Over **91,650 people** have been arrested on allegations of gang affiliation, representing roughly 1.5% of the country's total population.

The results on paper have been dramatic. The homicide rate, which stood at 53.1 per 100,000 in 2018, dropped to **1.9 per 100,000 in 2024**—a more than 98% decline. The country's major gangs, MS-13 and Barrio 18, appear to have been largely dismantled.

In March 2026, the Legislative Assembly approved a **constitutional amendment** allowing life imprisonment for convictions of murder, rape, or terrorism.

However, international human rights groups continue to raise serious concerns about arbitrary arrests, due process violations, severe prison overcrowding, and physical abuse of detainees.

## 3. Bitcoin & Economy

El Salvador continues its unconventional Bitcoin strategy despite pressure from the IMF. The government's "1 BTC per day" purchase program remains active, and as of mid-February 2026, the country's strategic Bitcoin reserve stands at **7,565 BTC**, valued at approximately **$635 million**.

This comes after the IMF required El Salvador to rescind Bitcoin's status as legal tender and wind down the state-backed Chivo wallet as conditions for a **$1.4 billion loan facility** agreed in late 2024. The Bukele government has technically complied on the legal tender front but continues purchasing Bitcoin, drawing criticism from the fund.

On a broader economic front, El Salvador's GDP growth is projected at around **4% for 2026**, buoyed by strong remittances, rising investment, and improved security conditions. The central bank has also diversified its reserves, acquiring **$50 million in gold**, bringing total gold holdings to 67,403 ounces (approximately $360 million).
