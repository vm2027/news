# Don’t Ban Teenagers from Social Media

*The Economist — Leaders | Let them scroll (Feb 14, 2026)*

## Core Thesis

Banning teenagers from social media would do more harm than good. The political consensus in favor of bans outpaces the evidence supporting them.

-----

## Context

- **Australia** banned under-16s from Facebook, Instagram, and TikTok (December 2025)
- **~12 countries** (UK, Spain, others) considering similar measures
- **70%+ of Britons** and **~66% of Americans** support under-16 bans
- Driven by high-profile tragedies and general anxiety about youth mental health

-----

## Why Bans Are a Bad Idea

### 1. The Evidence Is Weak

- Growing evidence that social media harms *some* children
- But the claim that it causes **mass mental health damage** to youth as a whole has only limited support

### 2. Enforcement Fails

- Teens in Australia already circumventing bans (e.g., manipulating facial appearance to appear older)
- Hard to define “social media” — WhatsApp and multiplayer games were excluded as too draconian
- Cyberbullying continues on unregulated platforms regardless

### 3. Unintended Consequences

- Banned kids may migrate to obscure, less-moderated platforms — greater predator risk
- Kids who bypass blocks less likely to report harm for fear of punishment
- Age limits just **delay** problems to age 16, when teens gain sudden full access with no experience
- Creates **false sense of security**
- Teens won’t suddenly pick up books — many will shift to games consoles and streaming

### 4. Ignores Real Benefits

- Vital for isolated youth (rural, LGBTQ+, neurodiverse)
- Broadens exposure to people, places, ideas
- Now a primary source of news and current affairs for young people

-----

## What Should Be Done Instead

|Action                             |Purpose                                          |
|-----------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
|Force platforms to share user data |Enable researchers to measure and address harms  |
|Eliminate addictive design features|Reduce endless scroll, autoplay                  |
|Require stronger content moderation|Protect young users from harmful content         |
|Improve age verification           |Enable tiered experiences (guardrails for minors)|

### Progress Already Happening

- **EU** issued preliminary ruling that TikTok’s design is “addictive” — threatened fines
- **US** blockbuster trials incoming from people harmed as children on platforms
- Major platforms creating **“teen accounts”** with additional safeguards

-----

## Closing Argument

> Bans are a way of ducking the care children deserve. If regulators can’t tame social media after 20+ years, what hope is there for governing AI? Youngsters have a right to share in new technologies — adults must make their time online as safe and rewarding as possible.
